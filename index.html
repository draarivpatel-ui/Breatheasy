          Why BreatheEasy

          About 4.8 million U.S. children (6.7%) have asthma right now, and 47% of them had at least one asthma attack in the past year (CDC, 2023 National Health Interview Survey). Doctors hand families a paper asthma action plan with green, yellow and red zones, but it rarely gets connected to tomorrow's air.

          That matters in Houston. The American Lung Association's State of the Air 2026 ranks the Houston-Pasadena metro 6th worst in the nation for ozone, with Harris County averaging 43.3 weighted unhealthy ozone days a year (2022–2024 data). EPA lists people with asthma and children and teenagers among the groups most sensitive to ozone.

          BreatheEasy puts the plan and the forecast on one screen: it computes tomorrow's AQI from the forecast using EPA's breakpoints, shows EPA's advice for that level, puts your doctor's own words next to it, and learns from your check-ins which conditions tend to come before your rough days.

          How the "AI" works

          A logistic-regression model trains in your browser on your own check-ins (a day is "rough" if you were in the yellow or red zone). Inputs: ozone AQI, PM2.5 AQI, pollen level, high temperature, humidity, and whether yesterday was rough. It shows its learned weights, explains each prediction feature by feature, and reports an honest held-out accuracy next to a naive baseline. It needs at least 14 days with some good and some rough days before it says anything. You can open any past day to see what it weighed, and a SYNTHETIC self-check on made-up people shows how well its percentages match reality and how it improves with more logged days. It finds patterns in your data; it does not diagnose and it is not a medical device.

          Limits

          • Forecasts are model estimates (Open-Meteo / Copernicus CAMS), not monitor readings. Check AirNow for official local forecasts and Ozone Action Days.
          • Open-Meteo has no pollen forecast for North America, so U.S. users log the Houston Health Department daily count themselves.
          • Guidance text is EPA's and NHLBI's general guidance. Treatment steps come only from the plan you type in from your doctor.

          Cómo funciona la "IA"

          Un modelo de regresión logística se entrena en tu navegador con tus propios registros (un día es "difícil" si estuviste en zona amarilla o roja). Muestra sus pesos, explica cada predicción y reporta una precisión honesta con días que no usó para entrenar. Necesita al menos 14 días antes de dar un pronóstico. Puedes abrir cualquier día pasado para ver qué tomó en cuenta, y una autoevaluación SINTÉTICA con personas inventadas muestra qué tan bien coinciden sus porcentajes con la realidad y cómo mejora con más días registrados. Encuentra patrones en tus datos; no diagnostica y no es un dispositivo médico.

          Límites

          • Los pronósticos son estimaciones de modelos (Open-Meteo / Copernicus CAMS), no lecturas de monitores. Consulta AirNow para pronósticos oficiales.
          • Open-Meteo no tiene pronóstico de polen para Norteamérica; en EE. UU. anotas tú el conteo diario del Departamento de Salud de Houston.
          • Los consejos son guías generales de la EPA y el NHLBI. Los pasos de tratamiento vienen solo del plan que escribes desde tu médico.
